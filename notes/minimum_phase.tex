\subsection{Group delay}
This subsection solves the Exercise 5.57~\cite{oppenheim2010discrete}. Consider the signal:
\[x[n] = s[n] \cos(\omega_0 n)\]
which has the following z-transform at $e^{i\omega}$:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        X(e^{i\omega})
        = \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n] \cos(\omega_0 n) e^{-i\omega n}
         & = \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n] \cdot \frac{e^{i\omega_0 n} + e^{-i\omega_0 n}}{2} \cdot e^{-i\omega n}
        \\ &= \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n] \cdot \frac{e^{i(-\omega + \omega_0) n} + e^{i(-\omega - \omega_0) n}}{2}
        \\ &\overset{*}{=} \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} \frac{s[n]}{2} e^{-i(\omega - \omega_0) n} + \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} \frac{s[n]}{2} e^{-i(\omega + \omega_0) n}
        \\ &= \frac{1}{2} (S(e^{i(\omega - \omega_0)}) + S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)}))
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:harmonic signal expansion}
\end{equation}
where we assume that the Fourier transform of $s[n]$ exists, so that in the step $*$ we are sure that both sums exist.

\begin{enumerate}[label = Assumption \arabic{enumi}, align = left]
    \item \label{eq:real signal assumption} $s[n]$ and $y[n]$ are real.
    \item \label{eq:concentration assumption} $S(e^{-i\omega}) = 0, \; |\omega| > \Delta$ for some small $\Delta:\Delta \ll \omega_0, \Delta \ll \pi - \omega_0$ \footnote{Those two inequalities ensure that the signal is spread enough so that it does not overlap with itself}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{pt:phase step} Consider the phase response as in Figure~\ref{fig:phase response shelf} and $|H(e^{i\omega})| = 1$.
          \begin{figure}[ht!]
              \centering
              \begin{tikzpicture}
                  \draw[->] (0, -4) -- (0, 4) node[right] {$\arg H(e^{i\omega})$};
                  \draw[->] (-4, 0) -- (4, 0) node[above] {$\omega$};

                  \draw (-3.14, -0.1) -- (-3.14, 0.1) node[above] {$-\pi$};
                  \draw (3.14, -0.1) -- (3.14, 0.1) node[above] {$\pi$};
                  \draw (0, 2) node[right] {$\phi_0$};
                  \draw (0, -2) node[left] {$-\phi_0$};

                  \draw[red, thick] (-3.14, 2) -- (0, 2) --  (0, -2) -- (3.14, -2);

              \end{tikzpicture}
              \caption{}
              \label{fig:phase response shelf}
          \end{figure}

          \begin{enumerate}[align = left]
              \item[``$\omega < 0$'':] \hfill

                    We have that $\omega - \omega_0 < -\omega_0 < -\Delta$, so by the \ref{eq:concentration assumption} we have that:
                    \[X(e^{i\omega}) = \frac{1}{2} S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)})\]

                    Thus:
                    \[Y(\omega)
                        = H(\omega) X(\omega)
                        = \frac{1}{2} e^{i\phi_0} S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)})\]
              \item[``$\omega \geq 0$'':] \hfill

                    We have that $\omega + \omega_0 > \omega_0 > \Delta$, so by the \ref{eq:concentration assumption} we have that:
                    \[X(e^{i\omega}) = \frac{1}{2} S(e^{i(\omega - \omega_0)})\]

                    Thus:
                    \[Y(\omega)
                        = H(\omega) X(\omega)
                        = \frac{1}{2} e^{-i\phi_0} S(e^{i(\omega - \omega_0)})\]
          \end{enumerate}

          We take the inverse Fourier transform:
          \begin{align*}
              2\pi \cdot y[k]
               & =
              \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} Y(\omega) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega}
              \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \left(
              \int_{-\pi}^{0} e^{i\phi_0} S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} +
              \int_{0}^{\pi} e^{-i\phi_0} S(e^{i(\omega - \omega_0)}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} \right)
              \\ &\overset{*}{=} \frac{1}{2} \left(
              \int_{-\pi + \omega_0}^{\omega_0} e^{i\phi_0} S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} e^{-i \omega_0 k} \dx{\omega} +
              \int_{-\omega_0}^{\pi - \omega_0} e^{-i\phi_0} S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} e^{i\omega_0 k} \dx{\omega} \right)
              \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \left(
              e^{-i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} \int_{-\pi + \omega_0}^{\omega_0}S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} +
              e^{i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} \int_{-\omega_0}^{\pi - \omega_0}  S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} \right)
          \end{align*}
          assuming $\Delta$ is small enough so that $\Delta \leq \pi - \omega_0$ we obtain that both integrals include the entire interval where $S(e^{i\omega})$ is non-zero hence we can extend them to $[-\pi, \pi]$:
          \begin{align*}
              \frac{1}{2} & \left(
              e^{-i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} \int_{-\pi + \omega_0}^{\omega_0}S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} +
              e^{i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} \int_{-\omega_0}^{\pi - \omega_0}  S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega} \right)
              \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \left(e^{-i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} + e^{i(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)} \right) \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}  S(e^{i\omega}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega}
              = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\cos(\omega_0 k - \phi_0) \cdot 2\pi \cdot s[k]
          \end{align*}
          dividing both sides by $2\pi$ we get:
          \[y[k] = s[k] \cos(\omega_0 k - \phi_0)\]
    \item \label{pt:phase step inclined} Consider the phase response as in Figure~\ref{fig:phase response shelf} and $|H(e^{i\omega})| = 1$.
          \begin{figure}[ht!]
              \centering
              \begin{tikzpicture}
                  \draw[->] (0, -4) -- (0, 4) node[right] {$\arg H(e^{i\omega})$};
                  \draw[->] (-4, 0) -- (4, 0) node[above] {$\omega$};

                  \draw (-3.14, -0.1) -- (-3.14, 0.1) node[above] {$-\pi$};
                  \draw (3.14, -0.1) -- (3.14, 0.1) node[above] {$\pi$};
                  \draw (0, 2) node[right] {$\phi_0$};
                  \draw (0, -2) node[left] {$-\phi_0$};

                  \draw[red, thick] (-3.14, 3) -- (0, 2) --  (0, -2) -- (3.14, -3) node[black, below] {slope $-n_d$};
                  \draw[dashed] (2, {-2 - 2 / 3.14}) -- (0, {-2 - 2 / 3.14}) node[left] {$-\phi_1$};
                  \draw[dashed] (2, {-2 - 2 / 3.14}) -- (2, 0) node[above] {$\omega_0$};
              \end{tikzpicture}
              \caption{}
              \label{fig:phase response inclined shelf}
          \end{figure}

          Thus:
          \[\arg H(\omega) =
              \begin{cases}
                  \phi_0 - n_d \omega, -\pi \leq \omega \leq 0 \\
                  -\phi_0  - n_d \omega, 0 < \omega \leq \pi
              \end{cases}\]

          Using the result from part~\ref{pt:phase step}:
          \[Y(\omega)
              = \begin{cases}
                  \frac{1}{2} e^{i(\phi_0 - n_d \omega)} S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)}), -\pi \leq \omega \leq 0 \\
                  \frac{1}{2} e^{i(-\phi_0 - n_d \omega)} S(e^{i(\omega - \omega_0)}), 0 < \omega \leq \pi
              \end{cases}\]

          By integrating the lower and upper halves of unit circle we get:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item Lower:
                    \begin{align*}
                        \int_{-\pi}^{0} Y(\omega) e^{i\omega k}  \dx{\omega}
                         & = \frac{1}{2} \int_{-\pi}^{0} e^{i(\phi_0 - n_d \omega)} S(e^{i(\omega + \omega_0)}) e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega}
                        \\ &\overset{*}{=} \frac{1}{2} \int_{-\pi + \omega_0}^{\omega_0} e^{i\phi_0} S(e^{i\omega}) e^{-i n_d (\omega - \omega_0)} e^{i(\omega - \omega_0) k} \dx{\omega}
                        \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \int_{-\pi + \omega_0}^{\omega_0} e^{i(\phi_0 + n_d \omega_0 - \omega_0 k)} S(e^{i\omega}) e^{-i n_d \omega} e^{i\omega k} \dx{\omega}
                        \\ &\overset{**}{=} \frac{1}{2} e^{i(\phi_0 + n_d \omega_0 - \omega_0 k)} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} S(e^{i\omega}) e^{-i n_d \omega} e^{i\omega k}  \dx{\omega}
                        \\ &\overset{***}{=} \frac{1}{2} e^{i(\phi_0 + n_d \omega_0 - \omega_0 k)} \cdot 2\pi \cdot s[k - n_d]
                    \end{align*}
                    where in $*$ we used the change of variables $\omega \to \omega - \omega_0$, in $**$ we used the assumption~\ref{eq:concentration assumption} and in $***$ we used the time shift property:
                    \[S(e^{i\omega}) e^{-i n_d \omega}
                        = \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n] e^{-i\omega n} e^{-i n_d \omega}
                        = \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n] e^{-i\omega (n+n_d)}
                        \overset{\dagger}{=} \sum_{n = -\infty}^{\infty} s[n - n_d] e^{-i\omega n}\]
                    where in $\dagger$ we did the change of variables $n \to n + n_d$.
              \item Doing the same procedure as above we get:
                    \[\int_{0}^{\pi} Y(\omega) e^{i\omega k}
                        = \frac{1}{2} e^{-i(\phi_0 + n_d \omega_0 - \omega_0 k)} \cdot 2\pi \cdot s[k - n_d]\]
          \end{itemize}

          By summing those two equation and dividing by $2\pi$ we obtain:
          \[y[k] = s[k - n_d] \cos(\omega_0 k - \phi_0 - n_d \omega_0)\]
          and by using line equation to express $-\phi_1 = -\phi_0 -\omega_0 n_d$ we obtain the equivalent form:
          \begin{equation} \label{eq:phase shift harmonic}
              y[n] = s[n - n_d] \cos(\omega_0 n - \phi_1)
          \end{equation}

    \item Since $x[n]$ is narrowband we have that $X(\omega)$ is non-zero in the neighbourhood $\omega_0 \pm \Delta$ and $-\omega_0 \pm \Delta$ so for the output signal we care only about those parts as $Y(\omega) = X(\omega) H(\omega)$. Using the basic property of z-transform $\bar{x} \ztransform \overline{X(\bar{z})}$ we get that for the real valued signal the phase is inverted when the argument is conjugated as $x = \bar{x} \ztransform \overline{X(\bar{z})} \Rightarrow X(\bar{z}) = \overline{X(\bar{\bar{z}})} = \overline{X(z)}$. As we assume both signals $x$ and $y$ to be real valued this property automatically holds for $H$

          In the neighbourhood of $\omega_0$ the linear approximation takes the form:
          \[\arg H(\omega) \approx \arg H(e^{i\omega_0}) + \partial_\omega(\arg H)(e^{i\omega_0}) \cdot (\omega - \omega_0)\]

          Define $\tau_{gr} = -\partial_\omega(\arg H) (e^{i\omega_0})$ and $\tau_{ph} = -(1 / \omega_0) \arg H(e^{i\omega_0})$. In terms of the Figure~\ref{fig:phase response inclined shelf} it becomes $-\phi_1 = \arg H(e^{i\omega_0}) = -\omega_0 \tau_{ph}$ and $\text{slope} = -n_d = -\tau_{gr} \Rightarrow n_d = \tau_{gr}$. Plugging this into \eqref{eq:phase shift harmonic}:
          \begin{equation} \label{eq:group delay}
              y[n] = s[n - n_d] \cos(\omega_0 n - \phi_1)
              = s[n - \tau_{gr}] \cos(\omega_0 n - \omega_0 \tau_{ph})
              = s[n - \tau_{gr}] \cos(\omega_0 (n -  \tau_{ph}))
          \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{All-pass} \label{sec:allpass}
\begin{definition}
    We define a single pole all-pass system:
    \[H_{ap}(z) = \frac{z^{-1} - \bar{a}}{1 - az^{-1}}\]
\end{definition}
\begin{lemma}[Properties of all-pass systems]
    Suppose $a = r e^{i \theta}$, where $0 \leq r < 1$. Then:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \label{lem:allpass const} $|H_{ap}(e^{i\omega})| = 1 \; \forall \omega \in [0, 2\pi]$
        \item \label{lem:allpass group delay} $\grd(\omega) = \frac{1 - r^2}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}$
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} \hfill
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item
              \[\frac{{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1} - \bar{a}}{1 - a{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1}}
                  = \frac{e^{-i\omega} - \bar{a}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}}
                  = e^{-i\omega}\frac{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}}\]
              Note that:
              \[\overline{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}} = 1 - a e^{-i\omega}\]
              so:
              \[\left|\frac{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}}\right| = 1\]
              thus:
              \[\left|e^{-i\omega}\frac{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}}\right|
                  =|e^{-i\omega}| \left|\frac{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}}\right|
                  = 1\]
        \item We compute:
              \[\arg \left(\frac{{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1} - \bar{a}}{1 - a{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1}} \right)
                  = \arg e^{-i\omega} + \arg \left(\frac{1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}}{1 - a e^{-i\omega}} \right)
                  = \arg e^{-i\omega} + 2 \arg{(1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega})}\]

              Then:
              \[1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega}
                  = 1 - r e^{-i \theta} e^{i\omega}
                  = 1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta) - i r \sin(\omega - \theta)\]

              Since $\real(1-\bar a e^{i\omega}) > 0$, the point lies in the right half-plane, where $\arg z = \arctan(\imag z / \real z)$:
              \[\arg{(1 - \bar{a} e^{i\omega})}
                  = \arctan \left(\frac{-r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)
                  = -\arctan \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)\]
              hence:
              \[\arg \left(\frac{{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1} - \bar{a}}{1 - a{(e^{i\omega})}^{-1}} \right)
                  = -\omega - 2\arctan \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)\]

              Now we compute the group delay:
              \begin{enumerate}[label = Step \arabic*., align = left]
                  \item
                        \begin{align*}
                            \partial_{\omega} \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)} \right)
                             & = \frac{r \cos(\omega - \theta) \cdot (1 - r \cos(\omega - \theta)) - r\sin(\omega - \theta) \cdot r\sin(\omega - \theta)}{(1-r\cos(\omega - \theta))^2}
                            \\ &= \frac{r \cos(\omega - \theta) - r^2 \cos^2(\omega - \theta) - r^2 \sin^2(\omega - \theta)}{(1-r\cos(\omega - \theta))^2}
                            \\ &= \frac{r \cos(\omega - \theta) - r^2}{(1-r\cos(\omega - \theta))^2}
                        \end{align*}
                  \item
                        \begin{align*}
                            1 + \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)} \right)^2
                             & = 1 + \frac{r^2 \sin^2(\omega - \theta)}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2 \cos^2 (\omega - \theta)}
                            \\ &= \frac{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2 \cos^2 (\omega - \theta) + r^2 \sin^2(\omega - \theta)}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2 \cos^2 (\omega - \theta)}
                            \\ &= \frac{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}{(1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta))^2}
                        \end{align*}
                  \item
                        \begin{align*}
                            \partial_{\omega} \arctan \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)
                             & = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)^2} \cdot \partial_{\omega} \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)
                            \\ &= \frac{(1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta))^2}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2} \cdot \frac{r \cos(\omega - \theta) - r^2}{(1 - r\cos(\omega - \theta))^2}
                            \\ &= \frac{r \cos(\omega - \theta) - r^2}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}
                        \end{align*}
                  \item
                        \begin{align*}
                            \partial_{\omega} \left( -\omega - 2\arctan \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right) \right)
                             & = -1 - 2 \cdot \partial_{\omega} \arctan \left(\frac{r \sin(\omega - \theta)}{1 - r \cos (\omega - \theta)}\right)
                            \\ &=-1 - \frac{2r \cos(\omega - \theta) - 2r^2}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}
                            \\ &=\frac{r^2 - 1}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}
                        \end{align*}
              \end{enumerate}

              Thus:
              \[\grd(\omega)
                  = - \partial_\omega{(\arg{H_{ap}(e^{i\omega})})}
                  = \frac{1 - r^2}{1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2}\]
              which is non-negative since $0 \leq r < 1$, hence $-2r \cos(\omega - \theta) \geq -2r$, so:
              \[1 - 2r \cos (\omega - \theta) + r^2
                  \geq 1 - 2r + r^2
                  = (1 - r)^2 > 0\]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

By cascading two all-pass filters with the conjugate poles we get:
\[H_{ap}(z)
    = \frac{z^{-1} - \bar{a}}{1 - az^{-1}} \frac{z^{-1} - a}{1 - \bar{a}z^{-1}}
    =  \frac{z^{-2} - (\bar{a} + a) z^{-1} + |a|^2}{1 - (\bar{a} + a) z^{-1} + |a|^2 z^{-2}}\]
with all the real coefficients as $a + \bar{a} = 2\real(a)$

By Lemma~\ref{lem:allpass group delay} we have that the group delay of the both parts is non-negative, hence the group delay of the whole system is non-negative as well as the phases add up. By Mean Value Theorem:
\[\frac{\arg H_{ap}(e^{i\omega}) - \arg H_{ap}(e^{i \cdot 0})}{\omega}
    = \partial_{\omega} \arg H_{ap}(e^{i\omega'}) \leq 0\]
for some $\omega' \in (0, \omega)$

\[H_{ap}(1) =
    \frac{1 - 2 \real(a) + |a|^2}{1 - 2\real{a} + |a|^2}
    = 1 \Rightarrow \arg H_{ap}(e^{i \cdot 0}) = 0\]
thus:
\[\arg H_{ap}(e^{i\omega}) = \omega \cdot \partial_{\omega} \arg H_{ap}(e^{i\omega'}) \leq 0 \; \forall \omega \in [0, \pi]\]

\subsection{All-pass decompostion}
Let $H$ be a stable causal system with real-valued coefficients. Then for any zero it holds that its conjugate must be zero as well. Suppose $H$ has a zero outside of the unit circle, namely at $z = 1/(c)$. Then it has a zero at $z = 1/(\bar{c})$ which we can factor out:
\begin{align*}
    H(z)
     & = \tilde{H}(z) \cdot (1 - 1/c \cdot z^{-1}) (1 - 1/\bar{c} \cdot z^{-1})
    \\ &= \frac{1}{|c|^2} \tilde{H}(z) \cdot (c - z^{-1}) (\bar{c} - z^{-1})
    \\ &= \frac{1}{|c|^2}\tilde{H}(z) \cdot \frac{1 - c z^{-1}}{1 - c z^{-1}}(c - z^{-1}) \cdot \frac{1 - \bar{c} z^{-1}}{1 - \bar{c} z^{-1}}(\bar{c} - z^{-1})
    \\ &= \frac{1}{|c|^2}\tilde{H}(z) \cdot (1 - c z^{-1}) (1 - \bar{c} z^{-1}) \cdot \frac{c - z^{-1}}{1 - \bar{c} z^{-1}} \cdot \frac{\bar{c} - z^{-1}}{1 - c z^{-1}}
\end{align*}

\begin{remark}
    The decomposition above holds for real-valued zeros too, but requires factoring out a single pole all-pass filter.
\end{remark}

We have split the original system into the cascade of the system where the zeros $1/{c}$ and $1/\bar{c}$ become $c$ and $\bar{c}$ and an all-pass system. By doing this for all the zeros outside of the unit-circle we get:
\[H = H_{min}(z) \cdot H_{ap}(z)\]
where $H_{ap}(z)$ is the cascade of the single all-pass filters. Using the result from Section~\ref{sec:allpass} the phase of $H_{ap}$ is non-positive, so $\arg H_{min}(e^{i\omega}) \geq \arg H(e^{i\omega}) \; \forall \omega \in [0, \pi]$, meaning that the phase delay $\tau_{ph} = -(1 / \omega_0) \arg H_{min}(e^{i\omega_0}) \leq -(1 / \omega_0) \arg H(e^{i\omega_0})$. With the same argumentation we can prove that the group delay decreases as well. When we fix the frequency reponse magnitude of the system we can reconstruct the original system up to its zeros. Since we can choose only from the original zeros of the system and the conjugate reciprocals (Section 5.4~\cite{oppenheim2010discrete}), we obtain the min-phase system by choosing only the zeros lying inside the unit circle. Otherwise with the argumentation above we can reduce phase and group delay by factoring out an all-pass system.
